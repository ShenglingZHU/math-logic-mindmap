## Motivation for combining

Connect the witness equation with the defining square-root property.

## Combination method and tools

Extract $\sqrt{2}=a/b$ from the witness statement and square both sides. Apply the square-root definition to replace $(\sqrt{2})^2$ by $2$. Finally multiply by $b^2$, which is allowed because $b>0$.

## Detailed derivation

### Continuous derivation

$$
\begin{aligned}
&\left\{\begin{aligned}
&a,b\in\mathbb{Z}\\
&b>0\\
&\gcd(a,b)=1\\
&\sqrt{2}=a/b
\end{aligned}\right.
\\[0.8em]
&\sqrt{2}=a/b
\\[0.8em]
&(\sqrt{2})^2=a^2/b^2
\\[2.5em]
&\left.\vphantom{\boxed{\displaystyle (\sqrt{2})^2=2\qquad (\text{Apply square root property, D1})}}\rule{0pt}{3em}\right\downarrow\qquad \boxed{\displaystyle (\sqrt{2})^2=2\qquad (\text{Apply square root property, D1})}
\\[2.5em]
&2=a^2/b^2
\\[0.8em]
&a^2=2b^2
\end{aligned}
$$

### Step notes

Extracting the quotient and squaring it are elementary transformations. The boxed definition supplies $(\sqrt{2})^2=2$; multiplying $2=a^2/b^2$ by $b^2$ uses the checked condition $b\ne0$.

## Combined result

$$
a^2=2b^2
$$

Square $\sqrt{2}=a/b$ using $b>0$ and substitute $(\sqrt{2})^2=2$ to obtain $a^2=2b^2$.
