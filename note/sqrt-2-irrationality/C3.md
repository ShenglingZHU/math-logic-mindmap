#### Input formula C3 K2

$$
\begin{aligned}
&\forall z\in\mathbb{Z},\\
&\quad 2\mid z^2\\
&\qquad\Rightarrow 2\mid z
\end{aligned}
$$

## Motivation for combining

Apply the parity theorem after the square equation establishes an even square.

## Combination method and tools

From $a^2=2b^2$ first infer $2\mid a^2$. The chosen numerator is an integer, so the boxed parity theorem applies to $a$.

### Step notes

The factor $2$ in the square equation gives the required even-square condition. Only the final step uses the external parity theorem, with $z=a\in\mathbb{Z}$.

## Combined result

$$
2\mid a
$$

| Symbol | Mathematical definition or role | Range / unit | Meaning / interpretation (if applicable) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\mid$ | Integer divisibility relation | Not applicable | Records evenness through divisibility by two |
| $a$ | Chosen numerator | Integer | Numerator of the reduced witness |

The even numerator is used in the denominator argument and final contradiction.

The square relation makes $a^2$ even. Since $a\in\mathbb{Z}$, the parity theorem gives $2\mid a$.
