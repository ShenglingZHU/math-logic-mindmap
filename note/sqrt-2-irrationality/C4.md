## Motivation for combining

Combine the square equation with the numerator's evenness.

## Combination method and tools

An even numerator has a square divisible by four. Use $a^2=2b^2$ to transfer that divisibility to $2b^2$, then cancel one factor of two to obtain $2\mid b^2$.

## Detailed derivation

### Continuous derivation

$$
\begin{aligned}
&a^2=2b^2
\\[2.5em]
&\left.\vphantom{\boxed{\displaystyle 2\mid a\qquad (\text{Use even numerator, R4})}}\rule{0pt}{3em}\right\downarrow\qquad \boxed{\displaystyle 2\mid a\qquad (\text{Use even numerator, R4})}
\\[2.5em]
&\left\{\begin{aligned}
&a^2=2b^2\\
&4\mid a^2
\end{aligned}\right.
\\[0.8em]
&4\mid 2b^2
\\[0.8em]
&2\mid b^2
\end{aligned}
$$

### Step notes

The boxed input establishes $2\mid a$. Squaring an even integer gives $4\mid a^2$; substitution into $a^2=2b^2$ gives $4\mid 2b^2$, which is equivalent to $2\mid b^2$ by integer divisibility.

## Combined result

$$
2\mid b^2
$$

| Symbol | Mathematical definition or role | Range / unit | Meaning / interpretation (if applicable) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\mid$ | Integer divisibility relation | Not applicable | Records evenness through divisibility by two |
| $b$ | Chosen positive denominator | Positive integer | Denominator of the reduced witness |

The denominator square is even, enabling the second parity-theorem application.

Because $a$ is even, its square is divisible by four. The equality $a^2=2b^2$ therefore implies $2\mid b^2$.
