\begin{aligned}

&\left.\vphantom{\boxed{\displaystyle 2\mid a\qquad (\text{Use even numerator, R4})}}\rule{0pt}{3em}\right\downarrow\qquad \boxed{\displaystyle 2\mid a\qquad (\text{Use even numerator, R4})}
\\[2.5em]
&\left\{\begin{aligned}

\end{aligned}\right.
\\[2.5em]
&\left.\vphantom{\boxed{\displaystyle 2\mid b\qquad (\text{Use even denominator, R6})}}\rule{0pt}{3em}\right\downarrow\qquad \boxed{\displaystyle 2\mid b\qquad (\text{Use even denominator, R6})}
\\[2.5em]
&\left\{\begin{aligned}
&\gcd(a,b)=1\\
&\gcd(a,b)\ge 2
\end{aligned}\right.
\end{aligned}
$$

## Combined result

$$
\left\{\begin{aligned}
&\gcd(a,b)=1\\
&\gcd(a,b)\ge 2
\end{aligned}\right.
$$

| Symbol | Mathematical definition or role | Range / unit | Meaning / interpretation (if applicable) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\gcd$ | Greatest common divisor | Positive integer on nonzero pairs | Tests whether a fraction is reduced |
| $a$ | Chosen numerator | Integer | Numerator of the reduced witness |
| $b$ | Chosen positive denominator | Positive integer | Denominator of the reduced witness |

The explicit incompatibility closes the witness scope.

The witness is coprime, yet $2\mid a$ and $2\mid b$ make two a common divisor, forcing $\gcd(a,b)\ge2$. This directly contradicts $\gcd(a,b)=1$.
