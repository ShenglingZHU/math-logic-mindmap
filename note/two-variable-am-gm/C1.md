## Motivation for combining

A square reveals the difference between the two squared sides.

## Combination method and tools

A1 gives $a,b\in\mathbb R$, so $t:=a-b\in\mathbb R$. Apply K1 with this substitution, expand, and add $4ab$ to both sides.

## Detailed derivation

### Continuous derivation

$$
\begin{aligned}
\forall t\in\mathbb R,\quad t^2&\ge0
\\[2.5em]
&\left.\vphantom{\boxed{\displaystyle a,b\in[0,\infty)\qquad (\text{Real-domain check, A1})}}\rule{0pt}{3em}\right\downarrow\qquad \boxed{\displaystyle a,b\in[0,\infty)\qquad (\text{Real-domain check, A1})}
\\[2.5em]
0&\le(a-b)^2
\\[0.8em]
0&\le(a+b)^2-4ab
\\[0.8em]
(a+b)^2&\ge 4ab
\end{aligned}
$$

### Step notes

At the A1 input step, $a,b\in[0,\infty)$ implies $a,b\in\mathbb R$ and therefore $t:=a-b\in\mathbb R$. K1 then yields $0\le(a-b)^2$. Expanding $(a-b)^2=(a+b)^2-4ab$ and adding $4ab$ to both sides gives the result. This squared comparison actually holds for any real $a,b$; their nonnegativity is essential in C2.

## Combined result

$$
(a+b)^2\ge 4ab
$$
