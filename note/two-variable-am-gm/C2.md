#### Input formula C2 K2

$$
\begin{aligned}&\forall u,v\in[0,\infty),\\&\quad u\le v\\&\qquad\Rightarrow\sqrt u\le\sqrt v\end{aligned}
$$

## Motivation for combining

The squared comparison is useful only after the nonnegative sides are identified.

## Combination method and tools

Verify the domain, apply square-root monotonicity, simplify principal roots, and divide by $2$.

### Step notes

A1 gives $ab\ge0$, $4ab\ge0$, and $a+b\ge0$; with R1 this gives $0\le4ab\le(a+b)^2$. K2 applies with $u:=4ab$ and $v:=(a+b)^2$. At the auxiliary box, A1 again supplies the nonnegative conditions. The principal square root is the unique nonnegative number with the specified square. Since $2\sqrt{ab}\ge0$ and $(2\sqrt{ab})^2=4ab$, its uniqueness gives $\sqrt{4ab}=2\sqrt{ab}$. Since $a+b\ge0$, the same definition gives $\sqrt{(a+b)^2}=a+b$. Substitute both identities into the main inequality. The final adjacent line divides by the positive number $2$, preserving the inequality direction.

## Combined result

$$
\frac{a+b}{2}\ge\sqrt{ab}
$$

## Conditions and verification

- Condition: $4ab\ge0$; check: A1 gives $a,b\ge0$, hence $4ab\ge0$.
- Condition: $(a+b)^2\ge0$; check: A1 gives $4ab\ge0$, and R1 gives $(a+b)^2\ge4ab\ge0$.
- Condition: $a+b\ge0$; check: A1 gives $a+b\ge0$, so its principal square root equals $a+b$.
