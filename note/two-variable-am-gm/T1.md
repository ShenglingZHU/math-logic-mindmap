## Equality case

In C1, adding $4ab$ preserves equality. A real square is zero exactly when its base is zero, so equality in R1 is equivalent to $(a-b)^2=0$, hence $a=b$. In C2 both compared quantities are nonnegative; the principal square root is injective there, so taking square roots preserves equality in both directions. A1 justifies the root identities in C2, and dividing by positive $2$ also preserves equality. Thus the target is an equality exactly when $a=b$.
